Accent on the fork

Distributary

An artwork proposal for Debian 14 “forky”

Colours

One river, many forks

A single stream enters from the left, splits, and splits again into a delta. Every junction is a fork. Debian is the river that much of the Linux world branches from, and for the release named Forky, the theme celebrates exactly that.

Distributary

In hydrology, a channel that forks away from a river and flows on toward the sea, the opposite of a tributary. Ubuntu, Raspberry Pi OS, Kali, Tails and hundreds more are built on Debian, and Mint on Ubuntu: a fork of a fork.

Fork

Every junction in the picture is a Y, and each split shares the flow between its branches by a generalised Murray's law. There are forks everywhere, without drawing cutlery or Forky, the Toy Story character.

Accent

The light sits on the first fork: the water is white where the river splits, then rose, magenta and violet as the delta spreads. On an HDR screen the fork is brighter than white.

Grown

A small program grows the river by the rules of real deltas, and faint contour lines turn the land between channels into islands. Run it yourself.

Boot splash

The river grows while Debian boots

The Plymouth theme shows the Debian logo with the wallpaper's own river under it. As boot progress advances, the water runs from the source and the river forks. It hurries through the stem, so the first fork is already lit by mid-boot, even when Plymouth quits before reporting 100%. It comes dark, with a light alternative; the Dark and Light switch at the top of the page shows either.

In use

On the desktop, and before it

Mock-ups drawn by the program around the theme's real wallpapers, login screen, GRUB background and installer banner.

In motion

From the source to the desktop

The river can draw itself in the order the water would reach it, and the whole start-up, from GRUB to the desktop, runs as one take.

Resolution independent

Every screen, sharp at 8K

Each screen shape is its own window onto one river, not a crop. The first fork stays in view even at 5:4, ultrawide gains a longer calm stem, and on phones the river flows down from the top.

High dynamic range

Brighter than white, only at the fork

The scene is rendered in linear light. SDR gets a tone curve that leaves everything below its knee untouched; HDR is the same picture plus exactly the light the curve removed, mastered for 1000 nits with SDR white at 203 nits (BT.2408).

Free software

Made by a program, free as Debian

Nothing here was drawn by hand, painted, photographed or made by an image-generating model. Every wallpaper, login screen, boot splash frame, GRUB background, installer banner, HDR master and video on this page comes out of one Python program, and you can run it yourself.

Source

The program is the artwork's source: its preferred form for modification, as the DFSG asks. Change a palette or the river's seed in design.py and every file follows. The pictures are build products, like binaries.

DFSG-free

GPL-2.0-or-later or CC-BY-SA-4.0, at your choice; the GPL is what desktop-base, Ceratopsian included, already uses. The Debian logo is LGPL-3.0-or-later or CC-BY-SA-3.0, and the “14 · FORKY” letters are Lato outlines (SIL OFL 1.1). All of it fits Debian main.

From Debian

Every tool and library it needs is packaged in Debian, and nothing is downloaded at build time.

Reproducible

The river grows from a fixed seed, so two builds give byte-identical files. make check also renders the SVGs in four renderers and compares them.

Fork your own wallpaper

In your browser, the palette editor recolours the river live or randomizes a variation of your own, and gives you the wallpaper as a PNG and the palette as JSON. To build the whole theme, clone the program and change design.py, or hand it that JSON.

salsa.debian.org/samueloph/distributary · github.com/samueloph/distributary

sudo apt install python3-numpy python3-scipy python3-cairo python3-gi gir1.2-rsvg-2.0 \
    python3-pil python3-contourpy python3-fonttools fonts-lato

git clone https://salsa.debian.org/samueloph/distributary.git
# or: git clone https://github.com/samueloph/distributary.git
cd distributary
./build.py svg                     # Forest, the default, as SVGs, in under a minute
SIZES=1920x1080 ./build.py raster  # and PNGs, dark and light, at your screen's size

That builds Forest as proposed, its dark version without the accent on the fork and its light one with it, into build/. --scheme builds a colour with the accent in both (forest, violet, midnight, red, teal, glacier, lineage or silt; add -even, as in forest-even, for any colour but Lineage without it), and --palette my-colours.json builds the editor's JSON. For the whole theme, use --draft all (up to 4K, 10–15 minutes) or all (up to 8K) instead of svg, and video for the animations (about a quarter of an hour, with ffmpeg); the HDR files also need libavif-bin, libjxl-tools and python3-openimageio. The theme is laid out like desktop-base.

Colours

One river, eight colours

Every colour comes dark and light, and all but Lineage with or without the accent on the fork; each is a complete theme. As proposed, dark comes without the accent and light with it; the switch at the top turns it on or off. Pick a colour here, or in the Colours bar at the top, and the whole page switches to it.

Without the accent on the fork, the water keeps one colour from the source to the first forks, and nothing is brighter than white. Colours that follow the river, like Lineage's red, are measured along the water from its source, so they land on the same channels on every screen shape.

Or fork your own wallpaper in the palette editor: start from any colour here, change anything, or randomize.
