Distributary

An artwork proposal for Debian 14 “forky”

Colours
Light on the fork

One river, many forks

A single stream enters from the left, splits, and splits again into a delta of light at dusk. Every junction is a fork. Debian is the river that most of the Linux world branches from, and for the release named Forky, the theme celebrates exactly that.

Distributary

In hydrology, a channel that forks away from a river and flows on toward the sea, the opposite of a tributary. Ubuntu, Mint, Raspberry Pi OS, Kali, Tails and hundreds more began as forks of Debian.

Fork

Every junction in the picture is a Y. There are forks everywhere, without drawing cutlery or Forky, the Toy Story character.

Light

The light sits on the first fork: the water is white where the river splits, then Debian's rose, magenta and violet as the delta spreads. On an HDR screen the fork is brighter than white.

Grown

Nothing is drawn by hand. A small program grows the river by the rules of real deltas, and faint contour lines turn the land between channels into islands. Run it yourself.

Boot splash

The river grows while Debian boots

The Plymouth theme shows the Debian logo with the wallpaper's own river under it. As boot progress advances, the water runs from the source and the river forks. It hurries through the stem, so the first fork is already lit by mid-boot, even when Plymouth quits before reporting 100%.

In use

On the desktop, and before it

In motion

The river, drawn and booted

The river can draw itself in the order the water would reach it, and the whole start-up, from GRUB to the desktop, runs as one take.

Resolution independent

Every screen, sharp at 8K

Each screen shape is its own window onto one river, not a crop. The first fork stays in view even at 5:4, ultrawide gains a longer calm stem, and on phones the river flows down from the top.

High dynamic range

Brighter than white, only at the fork

The scene is rendered in linear light. SDR gets a tone curve that leaves everything below its knee untouched; HDR is the same picture plus exactly the light the curve removed, mastered for 1000 nits with SDR white at 203 nits (BT.2408).

Free software

Made by a program, free as Debian

Nothing here was drawn by hand, painted, photographed or made by an image-generating model. Every wallpaper, login screen, boot splash frame, GRUB background, installer banner, HDR master and video on this page comes out of one Python program, and you can run it yourself.

Source

The program is the artwork's source: its preferred form for modification, as the DFSG asks. Change a palette or the river's seed in design.py and every file follows. The pictures are build products, like binaries.

DFSG-free

GPL-2.0-or-later or CC-BY-SA-4.0, the terms desktop-base already uses. The Debian logo is LGPL-3+ or CC-BY-SA-3.0, the “14 · FORKY” letters are Lato outlines (OFL 1.1). All of it fits Debian main.

From Debian

Every tool and library it needs is packaged in Debian, and nothing is downloaded at build time.

Reproducible

The river grows from a fixed seed, so two builds give byte-identical files; make check compares the SVGs in four renderers.

Fork your own wallpaper

In your browser, the palette editor recolours the river live and gives you the palette as JSON. From the source, clone the program, change design.py or hand it that JSON, and it builds the whole theme.

salsa.debian.org/samueloph/distributary · mirror on GitHub

sudo apt install python3-numpy python3-scipy python3-cairo python3-gi gir1.2-rsvg-2.0 \
    python3-pil python3-contourpy python3-fonttools fonts-lato
sudo apt install libavif-bin libjxl-tools python3-openimageio ffmpeg   # HDR and videos

git clone https://salsa.debian.org/samueloph/distributary.git
cd distributary
./build.py --draft all     # the whole theme up to 4K, in a few minutes
make                       # everything, up to 8K
make check                 # four SVG renderers, HDR signalling, videos
make video                 # the animations (slow)

The theme lands in build/distributary-theme/, laid out like desktop-base; ./build.py --palette my-colours.json --draft all builds one from the editor's JSON.

Colours

One river, eight colours

Every colour comes dark and light, with or without the light on the fork, and each is a complete theme. Pick one here, or in the Colours bar at the top, and the whole page shows itself in it.

Without the light on the fork, the water keeps one colour from the source to the first forks, and nothing is brighter than white. Colours that follow the river, like Lineage's red, are measured along the water from its source, so they land on the same channels on every screen shape.

Fork your own wallpaper in the palette editor. It redraws the river live in the browser from the same inputs as the program, and the palette it gives you builds into the whole theme.
